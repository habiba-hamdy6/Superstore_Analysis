Superstore Analysis

About the Project

This project is a business-focused analysis of the Superstore dataset.

I wanted to look beyond just Sales numbers and understand what the data is actually saying about the business — especially when it comes to Profit, Discounts, Products, and Customers.

Main Business Question

Does Discounting Actually Drive Profitable Growth?

I wanted to understand whether giving higher discounts actually helps the business make more profit, or if it can sometimes increase Sales while putting pressure on Profit.

Tools Used

- Excel
- Pivot Tables
- Data Analysis
- Data Visualization

How I Approached the Analysis

I started with a business question, then used the data to explore different angles and look for patterns.

Business Question → Data → Insights → Recommendations

I looked at:

- Sales vs. Profit over time
- Discount levels and Profit
- Product performance
- Geographical performance
- Customer segments and discounting
- Customer purchasing behavior

Key Insights

1. High Sales Don't Always Mean High Profit

Some periods had high Sales but much lower Profit.

This showed me that looking at Sales alone doesn't always tell the full story.

2. Higher Discounts Don't Necessarily Increase Profit

In the aggregated data I analyzed, discount levels of 30% and above were associated with negative Profit.

This made me look at Discounting differently.

The problem isn't necessarily the Discount itself, but how, when, and where it's used.

3. Some Products Generate High Sales but Weak or Negative Profit

Some Products were selling well but were still generating very low or even negative Profit.

So I drilled down into Products, Geography, and Discounts to understand where profitability was under pressure.

4. Not Every Customer Needs the Same Discount

Different Customers can have different buying behaviors.

Looking at Customer Segments and purchasing behavior made me question whether giving similar discount levels to different types of Customers is always the right approach.

Recommendations

Based on the analysis, I would:

- Set reasonable discount limits.
- Use Discounts more selectively.
- Consider the Product, Customer, and Order Value before offering a discount.
- Use Bundles and targeted offers instead of relying on large Discounts.
- Focus on Profitable Growth, not just higher Sales.

Final Takeaway

The data suggests that the problem isn't simply the Product.

Some Customers are willing to buy with little or no Discount, while some Products generate high Sales but weak or even negative Profit when heavily discounted.

So the goal isn't to simply sell more or remove Discounts.

It's about using the right Price and the right Offer for the right Product and Customer.

The goal isn't to sell more at any cost.
It's to turn Sales into Profitable Growth.
